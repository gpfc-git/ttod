Claim under test

The course does not grade “AI volume.” It grades corrective competence: can the human disclose help, verify the diff, reject bad advice, and defend ownership? That is the Unit 6 studio test — keep an ACCEPT/REJECT log and defend decisions — and the Practical Guide’s verification checklist.

Product Mean Σ on tip PRs can be high while AI-critical signs stay thin. This report separates those two surfaces on purpose.

Human ↔ AI boundary

Per-cohort reading

E1 Content — draft 9.0

Strongest alignment of product work and metacognition. Logs name mutation tests (break the test deliberately to prove it bites). M2 names the ritual explore → plan → wait for approval that caught stale task sheets. Design-decision #68 documents a rejected alternative with file/line evidence.

E2 Graph — draft 6.5

Tip Mean Σ matches Content, but AI-control signs are thinner: logs are present and verification commands listed, yet REJECT trails and M2 depth are lighter. M2 honesty (“hard to use AI as the main tool”) is useful data — it is not the same as demonstrated critique of model suggestions.

E3 Oracle — draft 6.0

Cumulative tip #42 includes a richer log schema (tool / verified / changed / uncertain). Moodle M2 is minimal. Product seam is complete; AI-critical narration did not keep pace.

E4 PWA — draft 9.0

Best process artifact in the cohort: docs/uso-ia-team-4.md (#74) with a concrete REJECT (drop tsc from the compatibility monitor). Multiple logs honestly state that the assistant ran checks the human did not claim — that is Verify, not a confession of failure.

E5 Accounts — draft 8.0

M2 describes the AI Review Log as the thing that forced thinking; gives a domain example (session cookie vs API token) where a working AI suggestion was refused for architectural fit. Tip scores slightly lower than peers; AI-control narrative is strong.

Moodle reflections — anonymized themes

What changed practice

  • AI Review Log as forced accept/reject thinking (E1, E5)
  • Plan-before-code vs stale assignment sheets (E1)
  • Mutation testing as verification theatre that proves tests bite (E1)
  • Bugbot reviewing AI-shaped plans — human still decides (E1)

What felt like paperwork

  • Repeating AI sections on tiny PRs (E1, E1 teammate)
  • Re-pasting context every session (E1)
  • Confusing / contradictory AI review bot comments (E5)
  • Wanting less AI overall (E3)

For your human qualification

  1. Treat draft AI 0–10 as a starting band; adjust with oral memory.
  2. Do not let tip Mean Σ overwrite Critique when signs are thin (E2, E3).
  3. Credit honesty about AI-run checks (E4) as Verify maturity.
  4. Enter Moodle grades from the companion conclusions note (private name map).
  5. Optional formative ask: one written REJECT from E2/E3 if you want parity of evidence.
